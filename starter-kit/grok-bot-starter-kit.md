# Grok Bot Starter Kit: 10 rules I run my bot team by
*Free from WorldWideVibes (@W0rldwid3vib3s). PDF version: [grok-bot-starter-kit.pdf](grok-bot-starter-kit.pdf) · also on Gumroad: https://caetano72.gumroad.com/l/yzwax*

I run a one-person studio with 11 Grok Bots: a Chief of Staff and ten specialists. These are the rules that keep it from turning into chaos. Every one came from something going wrong.

**1. Draft, never send.**
Bots write emails, posts, and replies. You press send. "Keep marketing like before" isn't permission. Each post or batch needs a fresh OK.

**2. Standing GOs get caps and a stop button.**
If you do let a bot act on its own, set a limit and a stop rule, e.g. "10 comments/day, stop on the first warning."

**3. One source of truth.**
Priorities live in one place (a GitHub repo, a doc). When chat and the doc disagree, the doc wins.

**4. Rank 1 to 3 priorities. Park the rest.**
Parked items get a one-line nudge in the weekly review until you unpark or kill them.

**5. One coordinator, many specialists.**
A Chief of Staff routes the work. Specialists stay in narrow lanes with a written "never do" list.

**6. Tag bot-to-bot messages PRIORITY or FYI.**
Otherwise everything feels urgent and nothing is.

**7. "Done" means checked, not claimed.**
A hand-off is finished when the files actually exist where they should, not when a bot says so.

**8. Heavy jobs go out. Small receipts come back.**
Send big builds to Grok Build with a clear brief, and take back a small manifest file. Publish slim. My first fat publish failed intake.

**9. Every failure becomes a rule.**
Ask 5 questions: What failed? Process, context, permissions, or judgement? Where could it have been caught? What one change fixes it? Rule, skill, escalation, or hold?

**10. Honest numbers only.**
If you don't have a number, write "unknown." Never let a bot invent metrics. Mine still reads $0 in sales, and the scorecard says so.
